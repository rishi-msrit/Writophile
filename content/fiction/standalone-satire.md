# The Last Committee

The Ministry of Administrative Oversight had, by the third year of its existence, convened forty-seven subcommittees.

The forty-eighth, formed on a Tuesday, was charged with determining how subcommittees ought to be formed.

Its first meeting lasted six hours and produced a twelve-page document titled "Preliminary Considerations Regarding the Structural Framework for the Formation of Subcommittees (Draft, Not for Distribution)." The document recommended forming a subcommittee to review it.

***

"We are making history," announced the Chairman at the second meeting, gesturing at the room with his pen. The room was a beige rectangle with fluorescent lights and a water cooler that made a sound every seven minutes regardless of whether anyone was thirsty. "No one has ever done this before."

"Formed a committee?" asked the Deputy Vice-Associate Liaison.

"Formed a committee to form committees."

"I believe the Parliamentary Committee on Parliamentary Committees—"

"Entirely different," the Chairman said. "They form committees for Parliament. We form committees for the Ministry. The scope is completely different."

The Deputy Vice-Associate Liaison wrote this down. She was not sure why she was writing it down. She had been writing things down for eleven years.

***

By the fifth meeting, there was a crisis.

The subcommittee had discovered, through careful deliberation, that it was itself a subcommittee, and therefore fell under its own jurisdiction.

"We must form a subcommittee to investigate this," said the Legal Advisor, who had been hired specifically to prevent exactly this kind of situation.

The motion passed.

"Who chairs the new subcommittee?" someone asked.

There was a long silence.

"I nominate the concept of accountability," said the Deputy Vice-Associate Liaison.

The motion failed.

***

The forty-ninth subcommittee's first act was to recommend dissolving the forty-eighth. The forty-eighth's last act was to form a subcommittee to appeal this recommendation.

That subcommittee has not yet met.

The Ministry, in the meantime, operates normally.
