---
title: "Chapter 1: Rust and Jasmine"
---

The Nilgiri hills loomed in the distance, dark and obscured by the gathering monsoon clouds. Below them, the air in Coimbatore felt heavy and warm, thick with the smell of damp soil and the impending rain.

Anbarasi Selvaraj adjusted the brim of her dark green baseball cap. She was holding her leather folder tightly against her chest to keep her hands from shaking. The folder bore the gold emblem of Tamil Nadu Agricultural University, her ticket into the Raghavan Estate. 

Looking at the towering iron gates, she felt a familiar prickle of anxiety. Anbarasi preferred plants and soil to people. She was shy, accustomed to blending into the background, but she had a stubborn streak when it came to her work.

She pushed the gate open. The rusted metal screeched against the stone path, the sound echoing through the quiet grounds. 

She walked down the gravel driveway, her sandals kicking up dust. The estate was silent, almost abandoned, surrounded by overgrown lawns. Anbarasi stopped by a wild jasmine bush near the path, her eyes caught by a metallic green jewel beetle climbing a leaf. 

She reached for the magnifying lens hanging from a cord around her neck. Squinting through the glass, she observed the beetle's shimmering shell. A small, genuine smile softened her face. At least the insects were still active here.

She let the lens drop back to her chest and looked toward the back of the property. 

The Victorian glasshouse stood there. It was a massive structure of rusted iron frames and clouded glass panels, looking like an old monument forgotten by time. Through the grime, she could see the dark, drooping shapes of the plants inside. 

Anbarasi walked toward it, her academic curiosity taking over her nerves. These were the rare hybrids bred by the late Mrs. Raghavan. If the university records were correct, they were dying.

She was three steps from the glasshouse doors when a voice stopped her cold.

"Idhu unga veedu nu nenaipingala? (Do you think this is your house?)"

Anbarasi jumped, nearly dropping her folder. She turned around quickly.

A young man stood on the stone steps of the main house. He was tall, easily six feet, with the athletic build of a tennis player. He wore a black t-shirt and dark trousers, his messy hair falling slightly over his forehead. He didn't look like a reclusive tycoon, but his face was set in a hard, hostile expression. He walked down the steps toward her, his movements steady but heavy.

"I... I have permission," Anbarasi said, her voice softer than she wanted it to be. She hated how easily she lost her footing when confronted. "I am Anbarasi Selvaraj. From TNAU. For the internship."

"Enakku adhai patri kavalai illai (I don't care about that)," Rudra said, stopping a few feet away. Up close, he looked exhausted, with dark circles under his eyes—the physical marks of a public doping scandal that had ruined his career. "My father signs agreements to keep up appearances. I don't. Leave before I call security."

Anbarasi swallowed hard. Her shyness urged her to turn and run, but she looked past him at the glasshouse.

"The plants are dying in there," she said, her voice finding a bit of strength. "The Crimson Orchid hybrids won't last another month. Your mother spent years breeding them. How can you just let them rot?"

Rudra’s expression went completely dark. The mention of his mother seemed to strike a raw nerve. He stepped closer, crowding her space. He smelled faintly of cedarwood and cold rain.

"You know nothing about my mother," he said, his voice dropping to a quiet, dangerous whisper. "Leave this estate."

"I have a legal university contract," Anbarasi said, standing her ground despite the urge to back away. "Your father gave us access. I have a right to be here."

Rudra let out a dry, bitter laugh. "A piece of paper doesn't protect you here. I decide who walks on this land."

Before she could respond, the clouds above them finally burst. 

It was a sudden, violent downpour, typical of the Coimbatore monsoon. The gravel path turned to red mud within seconds. Rudra swore under his breath. Without thinking, he grabbed Anbarasi by the wrist. His hand was warm and calloused, his grip firm. He pulled her under the narrow stone eaves of the glasshouse entrance, the only shelter nearby.

They were pressed close against the heavy iron doors of the greenhouse. Anbarasi could feel the cold metal behind her back and the heat of Rudra's body in front of her. The sound of the rain hammering the glass panels above was deafening.

"Kaiyai vidunga (Release my hand)," she whispered in Tamil, her voice trembling slightly.

Rudra looked down at her wrist, his fingers still wrapped around her skin. He didn't let go immediately. His dark eyes locked onto hers, observing the water droplets on her eyelashes and the warm, clay-colored tone of her skin in the gray light. 

Then, he slowly released his grip, his hand dropping to his side.

Before either of them could speak, a loud, heavy crash echoed from *inside* the locked glasshouse, followed by the sound of shattering glass. 

They both froze, staring at the locked iron doors.
