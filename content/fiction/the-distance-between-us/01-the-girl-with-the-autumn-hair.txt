Autumn Hair
I never gave much thought to love at first sight. It always felt like something out
of books or old folks' stories—until that morning in Ms. MacPherson's Advanced
Chemistry class. It had been raining all night, the kind of Highland rain that
never really stops, just fades in and out like a breath. And then she walked in.
Rebecca MacDonald. Late. Dripping. A mess of wet copper hair and energy that
seemed to pull every gaze in the room toward her. The rain had darkened her
hair to something richer, a shade between ember and mahogany, and droplets
clung to the ends like they weren't ready to let go.
"Sorry I'm late," she said, her accent carrying that soft Edinburgh lilt, lighter than
the rougher tones we had here in Invermuir. "Didn't think the rain would be this
bad."
She shook out her umbrella at the door, completely unaware that half the class
was watching. A few of the guys—Connor MacDougal, especially—leaned back in
their chairs, suddenly way too interested in the front of the room.
"Ah, Miss MacDonald," Ms. MacPherson peered at her over her glasses. "Our new
student. You can take a seat next to Mr. Mitchell."
My pencil snapped in half.
There were at least five other empty seats in this room. But no, fate—or
MacPherson—had chosen the one right beside me.
I hurriedly brushed the broken pencil pieces oﬀ my lap as Rebecca walked toward
me, completely unaware that she was turning my entire world upside down with
each step. The empty chair beside mine had never felt this significant.
She slid into the seat, smelled like rain and something warm—vanilla, maybe?—
and turned her hazel eyes on me.
"Hi," she whispered, like we were in on some secret. "I'm Rebecca."
I opened my mouth, aiming for something cool, maybe a simple "hey." Instead,
what came out was a strangled, "Hrng."
Brilliant. Absolutely brilliant.


She raised an eyebrow, biting back a smile. "And you are...?"
"Jamey," I blurted. "I mean, James. But people call me Jamey."
"Well, Jamey," she said, flipping open her notebook like this was the most normal
thing in the world, "mind if I borrow your textbook? Mine's stuck in transit."
I nodded. Too fast. Like an idiot. But when she scooted closer, her arm brushing
mine as we shared the book, I swear my heart stuttered. Across the room,
Connor's jaw tensed, and I caught a couple of other guys throwing glances our
way. I didn't care.
Rebecca wasn't just pretty. She had this kind of eﬀortless presence, the kind that
made a room shift without her even trying. But what really got me was how
easily she caught up with the lesson, her pencil gliding over her notes in neat,
confident strokes.
"So," she whispered, not looking up from her work, "what do people do for fun in
Invermuir?"
I almost laughed. Fun? Invermuir? Unless you counted watching sheep, loitering
at Old Tom's convenience store, or skipping rocks at the lake, the options were
pretty limited.
Before I could answer, Ms. MacPherson's voice cut through the room. "Mr.
Mitchell, since you and Miss MacDonald seem to have so much to discuss,
perhaps you'd like to explain to the class how ionic bonds form?"
Every head turned toward us. Rebecca bit her lip, barely holding back a grin.
For once, I didn't freeze. Chemistry was my thing, the one place I never second-
guessed myself. So I stood up and explained ionic bonding—electron transfers,
charge balancing, the whole deal. And when I was done, Ms. MacPherson nodded
approvingly.
But it wasn't her expression that stuck with me. It was Rebecca's. She was looking
at me—not like everyone else did, not like I was just another smart, quiet guy in
the back row—but like I was something worth noticing.
"Not bad, Mitchell," she murmured when I sat back down. "Not bad at all."
The rain kept tapping against the windows, the world outside unchanged. But
inside, sitting next to her, everything felt diﬀerent.
This was just another Tuesday.
Except... it wasn't.
