New
Jamey couldn't quite put his finger on when everything began to change, but his
friend Ethan always seemed to notice the little shifts. As they sat on the school
steps during lunch, Ethan casually remarked between bites,
"You've been spending a lot of time with Rebecca lately. Should I be concerned?"
Jamey rolled his eyes. "It's just tutoring."
"Sure, if you say so." Ethan grinned lightly.
Before Jamey could reply, an announcement crackled over the loudspeakers,
grabbing their attention.
"Attention students! The annual Pottery Competition is just around the corner.
Sign-ups close by the end of the day. Don't miss your chance to showcase your
skills!"
Jamey barely paid attention—until Rebecca plopped down beside him, grinning
like she was up to something.
"We're entering," she declared, sliding a sign-up sheet onto his lap.
Jamey stared at it. "We're what?"
She leaned in, her hazel eyes glinting with amusement. "Come on, genius. You
helped me with math; now it's my turn to teach you something."
"Pottery?" He scoﬀed. "I've never even—"
"Exactly," she interrupted. "Which is why you need me."
Ethan snickered. "Oh, this is gonna be good."
Later that evening, the art studio was empty except for them. The air smelled of
clay and faint traces of paint, the dim lighting casting a warm glow over
everything. Jamey sat stiﬄy on the pottery stool, eyeing the lump of clay in front
of him like it might attack.
Rebecca, on the other hand, moved with eﬀortless grace. Her hands worked the


wheel with confidence, shaping the clay as if it was second nature. Watching her,
Jamey almost forgot why he was there.
"You're good at this," he muttered.
She grinned. "Told you. Now, come here."
He hesitated, but she grabbed his hands and guided them over the cool, wet clay.
Her fingers slid over his, pressing lightly, directing his movements. Jamey tensed
at the sudden closeness, every nerve in his body suddenly aware of how near she
was.
"Just relax," she murmured, her voice soft and steady, warmth brushing against
his ear.
Easier said than done.
She didn't move away. Instead, she kept her hands over his, molding the clay
together, their fingers brushing with every movement. Time slowed, the steady
hum of the wheel filling the silence between them. Clay smeared over their
hands, warm and slick, but neither of them pulled away.
"There you go," she said after a while. "See? Not so bad."
Jamey exhaled a breath he hadn't realized he was holding. The clay had finally
begun to take shape—wobbly, imperfect, but still standing.
Rebecca wiped her hands on a rag and stretched, flashing him a satisfied smile.
"Not bad for your first time."
He shook his head, standing up. "Not bad? It looks like it barely survived a
storm."
She smirked, tossing him a clean towel. "That's what practice is for."
They packed up in comfortable silence, the moment slipping into something easy.
The next day, as they submitted their piece, Ryan Pierce walked up, flashing
Rebecca his signature, cocky grin.
"Didn't think you were the type to get your hands dirty, Jamey. Guess you're full
of surprises," Ryan said.
Rebecca smirked. "And you? Still too scared to try?"
Ryan chuckled, tapping the edge of the pottery stand. "Let's just say I know when
to pick my battles. You two seem to have it handled."


Jamey said nothing, but his grip tightened on the pottery stand. but instead of
responding, he let out a small breath, keeping his expression neutral. "We'll see
how it turns out."
Ryan lifted an eyebrow but didn't push further. With a shrug, he walked away,
leaving an odd tension hanging in the air between them.
That night, as Jamey lay in bed, he could still feel the ghost of her touch on his
hands, the warmth of her breath against his skin, refusing to fade.
And for the first time, he wondered if being invisible was really what he wanted
anymore.
