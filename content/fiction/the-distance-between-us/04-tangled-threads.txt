Jamey still wasn't sure how he got roped into this. One second, he was minding
his business; the next, Rebecca had pulled him into a pottery competition with
that all-too-familiar glint in eyes. Saying no was never really an option.
"Last chance to back out," she said, standing in front of their finished piece—a
slightly uneven but surprisingly decent-looking bowl.
Jamey sighed. "Not happening. If I suﬀered through all that clay on my hands, I
at least want a participation ribbon."
Rebecca smirked. "You're so easy to manipulate."
"Only when it's you."
She paused, something flickering across her expression before she turned away.
He didn't get a chance to think about it, though, because the judges were already
announcing the results.
Fifth place.
"All that eﬀort for fifth?" he muttered as they stepped away.
Rebecca shrugged. "It was never about winning," 
Jamey raised a brow. "Says the most competitive person I know."
"Okay, fine, but we made something together. That's what matters."
He smirked. "Now that's a line."
She rolled her eyes, but —something made his stomach feel weird. Something
that lingered when she looped her arm through his and said, "C'mon, let's get out
of here."
Things weren't the same after that. Rebecca had always been close, always been
playful—but now, there was something diﬀerent. Something more deliberate.
It was in the way she touched him. The way her fingers brushed his when she
handed him something, or the way she leaned just a little too close when they
talked. The way she'd find a reason to fix his collar or smooth down his hair, even
when it wasn't necessary.
It was messing with his head.


Ethan noticed first.
"You two have been acting weird," he said one afternoon.
Jamey frowned. "We're acting normal."
Ethan snorted. "Right. That's why you look like you're five seconds away from
malfunctioning every time she's around."
Jamey scowled. "I do not."
"You kinda do."
Jamey groaned. "It's nothing."
Ethan gave him a look that said he wasn't buying it. And honestly? Jamey wasn't
sure he was either.
The Party
A few days later, Rebecca cornered him after school. "Come to Ryan's party with
me."
Jamey blinked. "Ryan Pierce? The guy we both can't stand?"
She sighed. "Yes. But if I go alone, he'll think it means something. And I'd rather
not deal with that."
Jamey crossed his arms. "So I'm your human shield?"
She smirked. "Exactly."
He sighed. "What's in it for me?"
She stepped closer, her voice dropping. "You'll find out."
His brain short-circuited. Between the challenge in her eyes and the teasing lilt in
her voice, he knew one thing for certain—he was in trouble.
The day of the party, she showed up at his place to get ready together. Or, more
accurately, to make sure he didn't show up looking like, in her words, "a lost
college freshman."
"Seriously, Jamey?" she sighed, tugging at the sleeve of his hoodie. "You own real
clothes, right?"
"I am wearing real clothes."
Rebecca rolled her eyes, rifling through his closet before yanking out a fitted
black button-down. "This. Put this on."


She tugged at his sleeve, inspecting him with a critical eye. "You could at least
pretend to put in eﬀort."
Jamey rolled his eyes. "I did."
She huﬀed, reaching out to adjust his collar, smoothing the fabric between her
fingers. Her touch was brief, barely there, but it sent something skittering down
his spine. He stood still, suddenly hyperaware of how close she was, of the scent
of her perfume—light but unmistakable.
She tilted her head slightly, considering him. "Better," she murmured, more to
herself than him.
His throat felt dry. He wasn't sure what to say, so he settled for something safe.
"You know, some people might say you're a little controlling,"
She just smirked. "You're welcome."
When she was finally ready, Jamey thought he was prepared. Spoilers: he wasn't.
His heart missed a beat, stomach flipping in a way he didn't quite know how to
handle. He had seen Rebecca countless times. In class, at lunch, and during late-
night study sessions when she was barely awake and mumbling equations under
her breath. But this—this was diﬀerent. The kind of diﬀerent that made his throat
dry and his brain sluggish.
Her dress wasn't anything flashy, but it was the way she carried it. Her hair fell in
loose curls over one shoulder, framing her face in a way that made her look both
eﬀortlessly perfect and completely untouchable. And her lips—painted in a deep,
nude honey beige—made his thoughts go places they definitely shouldn't.
His heartbeat stuttered. And when she met his gaze, tilting her head slightly, he
knew she noticed.
"You're staring."
Jamey blinked, clearing his throat. "No, I'm not."
Rebecca just laughed, tossing him his jacket. "Let's go; it's getting late."
Ryan's house was packed. Music, flashing lights, people pressed too close. Jamey
hated it instantly.
"Remind me why I agreed to this?" He muttered.
Rebecca smirked, leaning in just enough that he could feel her breath against his
ear. "Because you can't say no to me."


Jamey scoﬀed. "Debatable."
She hummed, tilting her head. "Really? Then walk away."
Jamey stayed rooted to the spot. She grinned. "Thought so."
Before he could argue, Ryan appeared, smirking. "Didn't think this was your
scene, Jamey."
Jamey tensed. He didn't trust Ryan, didn't like the way he looked at Rebecca.
Rebecca seemed to notice because she suddenly looped her arm through Jamey's.
Her voice dropped, teasing but firm. "Dance with me."
Jamey narrowed his eyes. "That wasn't a request, was it?"
She tugged him toward the dance floor. "What do you think?"
Jamey wasn't a dancer. But with her, it didn't matter.
Her hands found his shoulders, fingers pressing lightly against the fabric of his
shirt as she pulled him in. Jamey's hands hovered at her waist for a split second
before settling there, heat seeping through the thin fabric. She was close—too
close.
Rebecca tilted her head up, her breath warm against his throat. "Relax."
Easy for her to say. His pulse was a goddamn riot, his thoughts tangled in the way
she felt against him, the way her fingers trailed lightly over his shoulders, tracing
the nape of his neck.
"Rebecca—"
She didn't let him finish. Instead, she leaned in, her breath feather-light against
his jaw. "You tense up every time I touch you," she whispered, her voice teasing
but softer this time. "It's kind of cute."
His jaw clenched. "Not cute."
She hummed, like she didn't believe him, her hands sliding up slightly, her
fingertips grazing the back of his neck. "You sure?"
He swallowed hard. "Positive."
Rebecca's smirk deepened. She shifted closer, her thigh brushing against his.
"Then why do you look like you might self-destruct?"
Jamey exhaled through his nose, tightening his grip on her waist. She gasped—


soft, barely audible—but he felt it. The way she tensed for just a second before
melting into him, the way her fingers curled slightly against his skin.
He smirked. "You were saying?"
Rebecca huﬀed, though it lacked any real annoyance. "I still think I'm winning
here."
"You think this is a game?"
She tilted her head, letting her lips barely graze his ear. "Isn't it?"
His breath stuttered. He wasn't sure how she managed to make everything feel
like both a challenge and a temptation all at once, but it was working. Too well.
Jamey turned his head just enough so their noses nearly brushed, their lips inches
apart. "You trust me, right?" she murmured.
His grip tightened. "Yeah."
Her fingers trailed down the back of his neck, sending a slow, burning shiver
through him. "Good," she whispered, her voice lower now
And then she pulled back, taking the heat with her, leaving him standing there—
breathless, wrecked, and completely at her mercy.
As they stepped outside to get some air, Rebecca leaned back against the railing,
stretching her arms above her head. The movement was eﬀortless, casual—yet
something about it made Jamey's pulse jump. She let out a soft sigh, tilting her
face toward the night sky, and for a second, he just watched her. The way the
dim light caught in her curls, the way her skin glowed under the streetlamps.
"Something on your mind?"
Jamey exhaled sharply, dragging a hand through his hair. "You're exhausting."
Rebecca laughed, low and teasing. "And yet, you're still here."
They left the party early. Neither of them spoke as they walked, the night air cool
against overheated skin.
Jamey's mind was still replaying everything. The way she had touched him. The
way she had looked at him. The way he hadn't wanted to let go.
Rebecca finally broke the silence, her voice quieter now, more serious. "Thanks
for coming with me."


"Yeah," he said, voice rough.
She hesitated, then took a step closer. "Jamey—"
He stopped breathing.
She exhaled, shaking her head. "Never mind. Goodnight."
Then she disappeared inside, leaving him standing on her porch, heart
hammering, pulse wrecked.
Everything was changing. And for the first time, he wasn't sure what scared him
more—the fact that things were shifting between them...
Or the fact that he wanted them to.
