---
title: "Chapter Fourteen: The Breaking Point"
date: "2026-07-20"
---

The weekend after the exams was a blur of silence and suspended breath.
Rebecca stayed inside her room, the door shut, listening to the quiet, heavy
movements of her mother downstairs. They didn't speak. Helen MacDonald had
ignored her since Friday afternoon, treating her presence like a stain on the
carpets she had spent the morning scrubbing.
By Monday afternoon, the silence had grown suﬀocating.
The final marks were posted on the school's bulletin board at three o'clock.
Rebecca walked down the corridor of Invermuir High, the air smelling of wet
coats and floor polish. Her throat was tight, her heart thumping against her ribs
in a rhythm that made her ears ring.
The corridor was crowded, students pushing past each other, some laughing,
others staring blankly at the floor.
She saw Jamey first.
He was leaning against the lockers opposite the board, his hands shoved deep into
his pockets, his hair messy. The moment she entered the hall, his head snapped
up.
He didn't smile—not yet—but the tension in his shoulders seemed to ease slightly.
Rebecca walked toward him, her legs feeling like lead. "Is it up?"
He nodded, gesturing toward the crowd. "Yeah."
"Did you look?" she whispered, her hands curling into the sleeves of her hoodie.
"Only for you," he said softly.
Rebecca took a breath, closing her eyes for a split second, before forcing herself
to look at the paper pinned under the glass frame.
Her name was halfway down the alphabet list.
*MacDonald, Rebecca.*
Beside it, in two neat columns under Mathematics and Advanced Chemistry:


*Grade: First Class.*
*Grade: First Class.*
Rebecca stared at the ink until the letters began to blur. The breath she had been
holding since Helen's return finally broke, a sharp, choked gasp escaping her
throat.
"You did it," Jamey murmured, stepping close enough that his shoulder pressed
against hers.
"I did," she whispered, a sudden, blinding relief washing over her. She turned,
looking up at him, her eyes bright with unshed tears. "Jamey, I passed. Both of
them."
"I told you," he said, the corners of his eyes crinkling. "You're too smart to fail."
He didn't care about the people around them. He reached out, his hand sliding
into hers, his fingers locking between hers with a firm, warm grip.
"Come on," he said quietly. "Let's go show her."
Rebecca's chest tightened, the relief faltering for a second. "Jamey... she's not
going to make it easy."
"I know," Jamey said, his jaw tightening. "Which is why I'm coming with you."
"No," she said quickly, stopping in the middle of the school lobby. "If she sees
you, it'll make it worse. She'll say you're the reason I'm—"
"I don't care what she says about me," Jamey interrupted, his gaze locking onto
hers, steady and unshakable. "But I'm not letting you face her alone. Not this
time."
Rebecca looked at their joined hands, feeling the solid, grounding warmth of his
fingers. For years, she had faced every battle alone, her walls built high to keep
the world out.
But looking at Jamey, she realized she didn't need the walls anymore.
She nodded. "Okay."
The walk to her house was silent. The Invermuir drizzle had returned, hanging in
the air like a cold mist, dampening their hair and clothes.
When they reached the gate, Jamey walked up the gravel path beside her, his
hand never letting go of hers.


Rebecca pushed the front door open.
The house was cold, the scent of lavender and bleach hanging heavy in the
hallway. Helen MacDonald was sitting at the kitchen table, a cup of tea in front of
her, her laptop open.
When she heard the door, she looked up. Her eyes fell first on Rebecca, then
shifted to Jamey, her gaze hardening into ice.
"You brought him here," Helen said, her voice low and venomous.
"I passed, Mom," Rebecca said, stepping into the kitchen. She reached into her
bag and pulled out the oﬃcial school mark sheet, laying it on the table.
Helen didn't look at the paper. "I told you I did not want this boy in my house."
"Look at the grades," Rebecca insisted, her voice trembling slightly but holding its
ground.
Slowly, Helen's eyes drifted to the paper. She scanned the two "First Class" marks.
For a fraction of a second, her jaw tensed, a flicker of surprise crossing her
features. But it vanished instantly, replaced by a cold, disapproving sneer.
"A fluke," Helen said, pushing the paper aside with a single finger. "You spend
months failing, and suddenly you produce first-class marks. Do you think Ms.
MacPherson is blind, Rebecca? Or did your tutor do the work for you?"
The insult hit Jamey, but he didn't flinch. His grip on Rebecca's hand tightened,
his thumb tracing slow, steady circles over her knuckles.
"I did the work," Rebecca said, her voice rising. "I studied every single night.
Jamey helped me understand it, but I wrote those papers."
"And I suppose you expect me to throw a party?" Helen stood up, her chair
scraping against the floor. She walked over to the counter, her back to them.
"You think a week of studying excuses a term of laziness? You think bringing a
local boy into my house, disrespecting my rules, and painting my walls is erased
by a couple of letters on a sheet of paper?"
"I spoke to Kenneth this morning," Helen continued, turning around, her face pale
with a cold, controlling fury. "Your train leaves on Friday. Your uncle has already
arranged your enrollment at the academy."
Rebecca's breath caught. The walls seemed to close in, the air in the kitchen
suddenly dry. "I passed. You said if I passed, I could stay."


"I said if you proved you could be a daughter I could be proud of," Helen
corrected, her voice dropping into that low, venomous register. "And you haven't.
You're unruly, Rebecca. You run out of this house in the middle of the night. You
ignore my calls. You spend your weekends with a boy whose parents probably
don't even know where he is."
"You're becoming exactly like your father," Helen spat, her eyes fixed on
Rebecca's face. "Running after distractions, thinking you can escape
responsibilities. I am your mother, and I will not have my authority mocked."
"Jamey, leave," Helen ordered, pointing toward the door. "Now."
Jamey didn't move. He stood right beside Rebecca, his shoulder pressed against
hers, his eyes fixed on Helen with a quiet, calm defiance.
"No," Jamey said, his voice quiet but carrying a weight that filled the room. "I'm
not leaving."
"How dare you—" Helen started, stepping forward.
"Mom, stop," Rebecca said.
The word was quiet, but it cut through the room like a blade.
Rebecca took a step forward, pulling her hand from Jamey's, though she kept him
close behind her. The trembling in her hands had stopped. The panic was gone,
replaced by a sudden, burning heat that rose from her chest.
"I am not my father," Rebecca said, her voice steady, echoing in the quiet kitchen.
Helen scoﬀed. "You have the same—"
"I stayed," Rebecca interrupted, her voice growing stronger, richer. "When he left,
I stayed. When you screamed at me, I stayed. When you called me useless and a
disappointment, I worked sixty hours a week at my desk trying to make you look
at me without disgust."
"You talk about my father running away," Rebecca said, her hazel eyes locking
onto her mother's, refusing to let go. "But you're the one who's running. You've
spent ten years hiding in your bitterness, using his mistakes as an excuse to make
me as lonely and miserable as you are."
Helen's face went completely white. "Rebecca—"
"You don't care about my grades," Rebecca said, the truth of it finally
crystallization in her mind. "You never did. If I failed, you got to punish me. If I
passed, you had to find another reason to control me. Because if I'm happy, if I'm


actually building a life here, it means you have to admit that you're the one who
is stuck."
"I work myself to the bone for you—" Helen began, her voice shrill.
"No," Rebecca cut her oﬀ, taking another step forward. "You work to keep your
walls up. You work so you don't have to face the fact that you've shut everyone
out. But I'm not going to live inside your walls anymore."
She gestured to the folder on the table.
"I passed those exams. I earned my place here. But I'm not staying in this house."
Rebecca turned, walking out of the kitchen and up the stairs.
Jamey followed her immediately, his heavy footsteps behind her on the carpet.
She pushed her bedroom door open, grabbed her duﬀel bag from the closet, and
began throwing her clothes inside. Her movements were quick, deliberate, free of
hesitation.
Jamey stood by the door, watching her. "Rebecca."
She stopped, holding a sweater, her breath shallow. She looked up at him, her
hazel eyes bright.
"Are you sure?" Jamey asked softly.
Rebecca walked over to him, dropping the sweater and cupping his face in her
hands.
"I've never been surer of anything in my life," she whispered. "I'm not letting her
take you away. I'm not letting her put a distance between us."
Jamey covered her hands with his, leaning down to press a firm, warm kiss to her
forehead. "Then let's go."
She packed the rest of her things in silence.
When they walked back down the stairs, Helen MacDonald was standing in the
hallway, her arms crossed over her chest, looking like a statue.
"If you walk out that door, Rebecca," Helen said, her voice shaking with a mixture
of anger and disbelief, "don't expect to come back. I won't support your rebellion."
Rebecca stopped at the bottom of the stairs, her bag in one hand, Jamey's hand in
the other.


She looked at her mother—really looked at her—and for the first time, she didn't
feel fear. She didn't feel anger. She only felt a quiet, heavy pity.
"I don't need your support," Rebecca said softly. "I just needed you to be my
mother. But you're too busy being his victim."
With that, Rebecca turned.
Jamey pushed the front door open, stepping out into the Invermuir drizzle.
Rebecca walked out behind him, and Jamey pulled the heavy wooden door shut,
the sound of the latch clicking into place echoing through the quiet street.
The rain fell around them, cold and stinging, but as Rebecca looked at Jamey, she
felt only the warmth of his hand in hers, the distance between them closed to
nothing.
