The latch on Rebecca's bedroom window gave a faint, metallic click as Jamey slid
the frame upward.
He moved with practiced slowness, stepping over the sill and into the darkened
room. Outside, the Invermuir wind rustled the leaves of the old oak tree; inside,
the silence was so thick it felt heavy.
Downstairs, Helen MacDonald was asleep, her presence a silent, suﬀocating
weight that seemed to seep through the floorboards.
Rebecca was sitting in the middle of her bed, her knees pulled to her chest, the
pale light of her desk lamp casting long shadows across her face.
When she saw him, she didn't speak. She just scrambled to the edge of the
mattress, throwing her arms around his neck the moment his feet touched the
carpet.
Jamey held her tight, burying his face in her copper hair. She was shivering, not
from the cold draft from the open window, but from the raw tension of the week.
"I thought you wouldn't make it," she whispered against his collarbone, her voice
barely a breath.
"I promised," he murmured back, his hands tracing the curve of her spine through
her thin cotton shirt. "I'm not leaving you to deal with tomorrow alone."
He pulled back slightly, his eyes searching hers in the dim light. Her hazel eyes
were wide, shadowed by sleeplessness, but there was a fierce, desperate look in
them that he had grown to adore.
They sat together on the edge of the bed. Rebecca's chemistry folder lay open
between them, the diagrams of carbon chains and covalent bonds looking like a
foreign language under the yellow lamp light.
"My hands won't stop shaking," she whispered, holding them out.
Jamey took her hands in his. They were ice-cold.
He brought them to his mouth, blowing his warm breath over her knuckles, then
rubbing them gently between his palms.
"You know the material, Rebecca," he said, his voice a low, grounding rumble.
"We've spent the last four nights going over every reaction. You scored a perfect


mark on the practice test today."
"I know," she muttered, looking down at their tangled fingers. "But when I think
about tomorrow... about the papers... I just see Edinburgh. I see the room my
uncle prepared. I see my mother packing my bags."
Jamey leaned forward, pressing his forehead against hers. "Look at me."
She did.
"Edinburgh doesn't exist tomorrow," he said, his gaze locking onto hers with a
fierce intensity. "Tomorrow is just a piece of paper. And you're going to destroy
it."
A small, watery smile touched her lips. "You're very confident."
"I've seen what you can do when you stop trying to protect yourself from failing,"
Jamey murmured.
He reached over, shutting the chemistry textbook and sliding it onto the floor.
The soft thud of the book seemed to signal a shift in the room, the academic
pressure fading into the background, leaving only the quiet, charged space
between them.
Jamey's hand moved to her cheek, his thumb brushing her lower lip.
"Let me help you forget about the exam," he whispered.
Rebecca didn't answer with words. She leaned in, her lips meeting his in a kiss
that was slow, reverent, and heavy with a quiet passion.
The danger of the house—of her mother sleeping just a floor below—added a
sharp, electric edge to every touch. They had to be silent, every movement
deliberate, every breath caught.
Jamey's hands moved to the hem of her shirt. He paused, his eyes asking the
question.
Rebecca nodded, her breath hitching.
Slowly, with an unhurried tenderness, he slid the fabric over her head, tossing it
aside.
In the dim light of the lamp, she looked fragile, her skin pale, her shoulders
slightly tensed. Jamey leaned down, pressing soft, open-mouthed kisses along her
collarbone, his fingers tracing the curve of her ribs, mapping the warmth of her
body.


He shifted behind her, pulling her back against his chest.
He kissed the nape of her neck, his lips tracing a slow path down the line of her
spine, his hands sliding over her hips, pulling her flush against him.
Rebecca let out a silent gasp, her head falling back against his shoulder. Her
hands found his thighs, her fingers gripping the fabric of his sweatpants as the
heat of his body rolled through her, melting the last of her anxiety.
There was no rush, no urgency.
In the quiet room, Jamey took his time, worshiping her skin, closing every
physical gap between them until the rest of the world ceased to exist.
They moved together in a silent, slow-burning rhythm, their breaths tangled,
their hearts beating in a chaotic, synchronized thrum. It was an escape, a quiet
rebellion against the shadow of Edinburgh, a promise sealed in the dark.
Afterward, they lay beneath the heavy wool blanket, their bodies tangled, the
warmth between them keeping the drafty room at bay.
Rebecca's head rested on Jamey's chest, her fingers tracing the red palm print
Jamey had painted on his chest days ago, now invisible but permanently etched
in her mind.
"Jamey?" she whispered into the dark.
"Hmm?"
"The distance between us," she murmured, her voice soft and reflective. "It's gone,
isn't it?"
Jamey turned onto his side, wrapping his arm around her waist, pulling her closer
until there was no space left.
"It's been gone for a long time," he whispered, kissing her forehead. "The miles
don't matter, Rebecca. Edinburgh, Invermuir... it doesn't change anything. I'm
yours. You're mine. That's the only equation that matters."
She let out a soft, contented breath, her chest rising and falling against his.
"Yeah," she whispered. "It is."
They fell asleep like that, holding onto each other as if they could keep the
morning from arriving.
But the morning came anyway, cold and wrapped in a thick Highland mist that


hung low over the streets of Invermuir.
Jamey had slipped out of her window before the first light of dawn, leaving only
a small, handwritten note on her desk: You're ready. I'll see you at the gates.
By nine o'clock, the courtyard of Invermuir High was filled with students, their
voices a quiet hum in the damp air.
Rebecca stood near the stone archway, her chemistry folder tucked tightly under
her arm. Her knuckles were white, her heart hammering against her ribs as she
stared at the heavy wooden doors of the school.
A hand slid into hers.
She looked up. Jamey was standing beside her, his expression calm, his eyes
warm and steady.
He didn't say anything, but the firm pressure of his hand was a lifeline, pulling
her out of the panic that threatened to drown her.
He pulled her gently toward the shadow of the brick wall, away from the passing
crowd.
Rebecca looked at him, her hazel eyes glassy. "Jamey—"
"Don't," he whispered, his hands coming up to frame her face.
He leaned down, pressing a soft, lingering kiss to her lips. It wasn't the passionate
touch of the bedroom, but a quiet, rock-solid promise that settled deep in her
chest.
He pulled back, his thumbs brushing her cheeks.
"You've got this," he whispered.
Rebecca took a deep, steady breath. The panic was still there, but it was no
longer overwhelming.
She nodded. "I've got this."
She turned and walked toward the school doors, her head held high, leaving the
distance behind her.
