---
title: "Chapter Five: A Breath Away"
date: "2026-07-17"
---

He stood outside her house, staring at the door she had just closed behind her.
The night air was crisp, but it did nothing to cool the heat thrumming beneath his
skin. He exhaled sharply, running a hand through his hair, torn between walking
away and knocking again.
Rebecca had always known how to play the game. She had spent years perfecting
the art of subtle control, of letting people believe they had power while she
pulled the strings. But this—this was diﬀerent.
She hadn't pushed him away. Not really. The hesitation in her voice, the way her
eyes lingered on his before she turned, told him everything.
He wasn't supposed to get under her skin. He wasn't supposed to matter.
Yet, here he was, still standing on her porch, pulse wrecked.
With a frustrated sigh, he finally turned and started walking. But as he moved
down the quiet street, he didn't go far. Instead, he leaned against a tree across
from her house, glancing up at her bedroom window. He could just make out the
faint glow of light filtering through the curtains.
She was awake. And he wasn't going anywhere.
The knock on her door came twenty minutes later. Soft but insistent. Rebecca
knew who it was before she even reached for the handle.
When she opened it, he was leaning against the frame, arms crossed, a stubborn
look in his eyes.
"You left me standing out there," he said.
She tilted her head. "And yet, here you are."
"Yeah, because I'm not walking away from this." His gaze searched hers. "From
you."
Something unsteady shifted inside her, but she masked it with a smirk. "Sounds
like a you problem."
He huﬀed a laugh, stepping closer. "Rebecca."
She didn't stop him when he reached for her, fingers wrapping softly around her
wrist. His touch was warm, grounding. Dangerous.


"I'm not playing this game with you," he murmured.
Her breath hitched. "Then what are you doing?"
His thumb brushed over her pulse. "Giving you a choice."
A choice.
She could step back. Close the door. Pretend this was nothing more than a
fleeting moment.
Or she could let him in.
Rebecca inhaled slowly, then, without another word, stepped outside, letting the
door click shut behind her.
"Come on," she said, grabbing his hand.
He blinked. "Where?"
"Somewhere quieter. Somewhere that's actually us."
Without waiting for an answer, she led him away from the house, their footsteps
quiet against the pavement. The city noise faded as they moved further from the
main streets, climbing a winding path up the nearby hill. The overlook wasn't far
—just high enough to feel separate from everything below. The city stretched
beneath them, a million tiny lights flickering like distant stars.
They lay on the grass, the night air crisp against their skin. She exhaled, watching
her breath disappear into the darkness.
"This is better," she murmured.
He turned his head toward her, studying the way the moonlight danced along
her. "Yeah. It is."
Her fingers found his, lacing together in the silence. It wasn't a game here. It
wasn't a performance. Just them, beneath an endless sky, with nothing to hide
behind.
The night had been perfect, filled with laughter, teasing remarks, and stolen
kisses that melted into something deeper. He had looked at her like she was his
entire world, and for a fleeting second, she had wanted to believe it.
She turned away slightly, watching the soft glow of the city lights below. He
shifted beside her, his warmth was intoxicating, a dangerous comfort that she
knew she shouldn't crave.


"You're quiet," he murmured against her skin.
Rebecca turned to him with a playful smirk. "For once. Don't get used to it."
He chuckled, his hand brushing along her arm. "I wouldn't dare. But I like this
too. Seeing you like this."
She tilted her head, feigning innocence. "Like what?"
"Soft. Comfortable. Happy."
Her heart stumbled over itself. She nudged his shoulder. "I'm always happy.
Haven't you noticed? I'm practically a ball of sunshine."
He grinned, "Oh yeah, Rebecca, the embodiment of pure joy."
She gasped in mock oﬀense, shifting on top of him, her hands pressed against his
chest. "You wound me. You know what? I take back all the sweet things I was
going to say."
His arms slid around her waist, pulling her closer. "Sweet things? Now I'm
curious."
She hesitated for a fraction of a second, then flicked his nose. "Too bad. You lost
your chance."
He laughed, catching her hand in his. "I'll earn it back."
The ease between them sent a flutter through her chest—an unfamiliar kind of
warmth. She had been with people before, but it was always calculated, always
part of a plan. But with him... it just was.
She released a dramatic breath, tilting her head back slightly. "Fine. I'll give you
another chance. But only because you look ridiculously cute when you're half
asleep."
Rebecca stretched, her jacket slipping oﬀ her shoulders. The way his gaze
lingered, warm and unreadable, sent a pleasant shiver down her spine. He
reached up, tracing a slow path along her collarbone.
"You really like this, don't you?" he murmured," "Us."
She stilled for half a second before recovering with a teasing grin. "Who wouldn't
like being adored? I thrive on it."
He hummed, unconvinced but amused. "Right. Of course. That's all this is."


Her smile faltered just a little. Was it?
Before she could dwell on it, he shifted beneath her, rolling them so that she was
on her back, trapped beneath his weight. His lips hovered just above hers, his
breath warm and teasing.
"I think you're scared," he whispered. "That's why you keep joking."
She opened her mouth, but nothing came out. His gaze softened, and instead of
pressing for an answer, he kissed her, slow and deep.
She melted into him, fingers tangling in his hair. He tasted like warmth and late-
night secrets, like something she was dangerously close to wanting for real.
When he pulled away, he rested his forehead against hers. "You don't have to
pretend with me."
Rebecca's heart thudded against her ribs. She should brush it oﬀ, laugh, turn it
into another joke. But she found herself whispering instead, "I know."
The silence stretched between them, comfortable and charged at the same time.
Then he grinned, shifting to the side, breaking the moment before it could settle
too deep.
"So... what was the sweet thing you were going to say earlier?" he teased.
She groaned, throwing a handful of grass at him. "Ugh, you ruin everything."
He caught her wrist, laughing. "That's a no, huh?"
She huﬀed, rolling over to straddle him again, her hands pressing lightly against
his chest. "Fine. You win."
He smirked. "I usually do."
She leaned down, her lips barely brushing against his ear, her breath warm
against his skin. "I was going to say... you make me feel diﬀerent. Like maybe,
just maybe, this isn't a game anymore."
His breath hitched. Their eyes met, locked in something heavier than words. And
then, without hesitation, she pulled him down into a kiss—slow, deep, and
consuming. His lips moved with unhurried precision, tasting, lingering, as though
memorizing every second. The softness of it sent a tremor through her, the
sensation sinking beneath her skin. His hand trailed up, fingers threading through
her hair, holding her there, but not demanding, only asking. She answered in
kind, her hands sliding along the curve of his shoulders, feeling the warmth
beneath his shirt, the way his heartbeat thrummed in sync with hers.


The world faded away—just the whisper of the wind, the distant hum of the city
below, and the intoxicating pressure of his mouth moving against hers. He tasted
like the night itself, like something unspoken and endless. When he finally pulled
away, their breaths tangled in the space between them, her forehead pressing
lightly against his.
She laughed softly, still caught in the moment, the weight of it settling
somewhere deep in her chest.
Maybe she wasn't just playing this time. Maybe she wasn't playing anymore.
Maybe she didn't want to.
