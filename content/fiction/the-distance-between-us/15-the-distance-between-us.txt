between Us
The gravel path up the hill overlook was steep, slick with the remnants of the
storm, but Rebecca walked with a light, unhurried stride.
Her duﬀel bag bumped against Jamey's shoulder where he carried it, his other
hand firmly clasped around her fingers. The air was cool, smelling of damp
heather, pine needles, and the sharp, clean scent of the Highlands after a
downpour.
Above them, the heavy gray clouds that had choked Invermuir for days were
finally beginning to fray.
A tear in the overcast sky let a shaft of late-afternoon sunlight spill through,
painting the edges of the hills in a rich, liquid gold. Below, the waters of the loch
shone like a sheet of hammered brass, reflecting the clearing sky.
When they reached the summit, Jamey stopped.
He set her bag down on the stone bench and took oﬀ his heavy waxed jacket,
spreading it over the dry patch of grass beneath the old oak tree.
"Sit," he murmured, pulling her down beside him.
Rebecca leaned back against the trunk, drawing her knees up to her chest. She
looked out over the valley, watching the tiny lights of the town begin to flicker
on like early stars.
The cold, hollow panic that had lived in her chest for as long as she could
remember was gone, replaced by a quiet, vibrating silence.
"What happens now?" she asked softly.
Jamey shifted, sitting close enough that his warmth seeped through her sleeve.
He reached out, his fingers catching a stray copper curl that had escaped her hair
tie, tucking it behind her ear.
"Whatever we want," Jamey said, his voice a steady, grounding presence. "You're
staying with me. My parents... they like you, Rebecca. They know what's been
going on. The guest room is yours for as long as you need it."
"And school?"


"We finish the term," Jamey said. "We go to Ms. MacPherson's classes. We do the
chemistry labs. And when the summer comes, we figure out university. Together."
Rebecca looked at him, her hazel eyes reflecting the golden light of the sunset.
For the first time in her life, the future didn't look like a dark corridor she was
being dragged down. It looked like an open field.
"I had the dream again last night," she whispered, her fingers tracing the edge of
his jacket.
Jamey stilled. "The drowning one?"
"Yeah," she said. "But it was diﬀerent. I was under the water, and the dark was all
around me, pulling me down. I saw the hands reaching for me, just like always."
She turned her head, looking directly into his dark, steady eyes.
"But this time, when they pulled me out, I didn't look at a stranger. I saw you.
And when I reached the surface, the water wasn't cold anymore. It was warm. I
wasn't gasping for air, Jamey. I was just... breathing."
Jamey's expression softened, a look of quiet reverence filling his eyes. He leaned
forward, cupping her cheek with his palm.
"You're not drowning anymore, Rebecca," he whispered. "You're safe."
"I know," she breathed.
She reached up, her hands finding the hem of his faded shirt, her fingers
tightening on the fabric. The proximity between them was no longer a shield
against the world, or a distraction from an exam. It was their reality.
Jamey leaned down, his lips meeting his in a kiss that was slow, deep, and filled
with an unhurried tenderness.
The Highland wind rustled the leaves of the oak tree above them, but on the grass
beneath Jamey's jacket, the world had shrunk to the heat of their skin and the
steady rhythm of their breathing.
Slowly, Jamey's hands moved under her hoodie, his warm palms finding the bare
skin of her waist. A soft shiver ran through her, her lips parting as she leaned into
his touch.
There was no rush, no shadow of Edinburgh, no looming threat of a ticking clock.
Jamey sat back, his eyes scanning her face, looking for any trace of hesitation.


Rebecca met his gaze, her hands moving to the zipper of her hoodie. With slow,
deliberate movements, she slid it oﬀ, letting it drop to the grass. She reached for
the hem of her shirt, pulling it over her head, exposing her skin to the cool
mountain air and the warm golden light of the setting sun.
Jamey's breath caught. He reached out, his fingers grazing her shoulder, tracing
the path down to her collarbone where the faint blue paint had been washed
away, leaving only the soft warmth of her skin.
"You're beautiful," he whispered, his voice thick with emotion.
Rebecca smiled, a genuine, radiant thing. "Come here."
She pulled him down, and Jamey shed his own shirt, his bare chest pressing
against hers. The sensation of his skin—warm, solid, and freckled—was an
anchor, grounding her in the present.
They lay back onto the jacket, the damp grass framing them.
Jamey took his time, his lips mapping every inch of her body with a slow,
reverent passion. He kissed her shoulders, the hollow of her throat, the curve of
her ribs, his hands sliding down her hips, undressing her completely under the
vast, clearing Highland sky.
Rebecca arched into his touch, her fingers tangling in his hair, her breath hitching
as a sweet, heavy heat pooled in her stomach.
For the first time, she felt no fear.
She had spent her life believing that giving herself to someone meant losing her
power, that vulnerability was a weakness that would leave her broken. But with
Jamey's body over hers, his eyes locked onto hers with absolute devotion, she felt
only strength.
She was not hiding. She was not running.
"Jamey," she gasped, her hips rising to meet his.
"I've got you," he whispered against her lips. "Always."
When they finally came together, it was a slow, beautiful union, free of the
desperation that had marked their earlier encounters. It was a celebration of the
freedom they had won, a silent vow sealed in the golden twilight.
Every movement was unhurried, every touch a language they had spent weeks
learning. They moved in a synchronized, passionate rhythm, their skin slick with
heat, their sighs swallowed by the wind.


The stars began to emerge through the fading light, tiny silver sparks scattered
across the deep violet dome above them.
When the peak came, it was a wave of pure warmth that washed over her, a
bright, blinding light that dissolved the last remnants of the walls she had built
around her heart. She clung to him, her face buried in his neck, her voice
catching on a soft, broken cry as Jamey held her flush against him, his own
breath ragged against her ear.
They lay together for a long time afterward, wrapped in the heavy wool blanket
Jamey had brought in her bag.
The sky had turned to a deep, dark violet, the stars shining with a brilliant, cold
clarity. Below them, the lights of Invermuir looked like a quiet constellation,
reflecting in the dark mirror of the loch.
Rebecca rested her head on Jamey's chest, her fingers tracing the steady, calm
beat of his heart.
"Jamey?"
"Yeah?"
"The distance between us," she murmured, looking out at the vast space between
the hills and the water.
Jamey wrapped his arms tighter around her, pulling her close enough that she
could feel the vibration of his voice in his chest.
"What about it?"
Rebecca smiled, a deep, peaceful warmth filling her soul. She reached up, locking
her fingers with his, pressing their joined hands against his chest.
"It's zero," she whispered.
And as Jamey kissed the top of her head, the stars shining bright above the
Highlands, Rebecca finally knew what it meant to be home.
