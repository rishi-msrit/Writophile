---

Jamey had spent years perfecting the art of being invisible.
Rebecca had diﬀerent plans, though!
Wherever he went, she was there. In class, tapping her pencil against her desk. In
the cafeteria, surrounded by people yet somehow separate from them. Even in the
town square, where he once spotted her sitting alone on a stone bench, gazing at
the twilight like she was waiting for something.
But he never thought she'd actually notice him again.
Then, one afternoon, she did.
"Hey! You're Jamey, right?"
He froze midway and turned slowly. There she was, standing too close, smirking
like she knew something he didn't.
"Uh... yeah," he said, gripping his books like they might actually hold him
standing against her suddend call.
"I heard you're a genius or something," she said, tilting her head. "Think you
could help me not fail maths?"
Jamey blinked. Was this some kind of joke? Rebecca, asking him for help?
But she wasn't laughing. She was waiting.
And in that moment, Jamey realized two things:
One: She was completely serious.
And two: His quiet, predictable life was about to get a whole lot more
complicated.
Rebecca was many things—sharp, confident, the kind of girl who carried the
whole world like it was hers for the taking. But when it came to numbers, she
was hopeless.
"I swear, these equations are written in some lost ancient language, Dropping her
forehead onto the library table, she said,  "Tell me honestly, Jamey. Do you think
I'm doomed?"


He bit back a smile. "It's just algebra. You're not doomed."
She peeked up at him, her hazel eyes narrowing. "That sounds like something a
genius would say."
Jamey shrugged. "I mean, you got this part right." He tapped her notebook, where
she had actually managed to solve one problem correctly. "You just overthink the
next steps."
She sighed dramatically. "That's because math hates me."
He shook his head, fighting the urge to laugh. "Math is neutral. It doesn't hate
anyone."
"Spoken like a true nerd," she teased, nudging his arm playfully. Her fingers
lingered for just a second too long, warm against his skin. Jamey swallowed hard,
his body hyper-aware of the tiny space between them, the scent of vanilla and
rain clinging to her sweater.
He knew he should have flinched, should have recoiled into his usual bubble of
personal space. But he didn't. Instead, he let himself enjoy the warmth of her
touch, the way she made the sterile quiet of the library feel alive.
"You're good at this," she admitted after a moment, twirling her pencil between
her fingers. "Teaching, I mean. You ever thought about tutoring?"
He hesitated, but Rebecca was watching him—really watching him, like she was
searching for something in him. Her eyes, sharp and curious, locked onto his, and
for a moment, Jamey forgot how to breathe.
"You should." She smiled, but there was something softer in her expression now.
"You make it... less awful, you know..."
For Jamey, who had spent most of his life being overlooked, it was an odd thing
to be seen so clearly by someone like Rebecca.
And he wasn't sure yet if it terrified him or made him want more.
The library had emptied out by the time they finally packed up their books. The
air between them felt diﬀerent now—not awkward, but charged, like an
unspoken challenge hung between them.
"So," Rebecca said as they walked toward the exit, "since you're my oﬃcial math
savior, I owe you. Coﬀee?"
Jamey's brain short-circuited for a second. "Coﬀee?"


"Yeah. You drink it, right? Or is that too mainstream for geniuses?"
He huﬀed a laugh. "I drink coﬀee."
"Good," she said, stepping into the cold night air. "Because I make a terrible
student, but I do know a thing or two about bribery."
She started walking backward, her hair catching the glow of the streetlights.
"Meet me tomorrow? After school?"
Jamey should've said no. Should've reminded her that people like her didn't hang
out with people like him. But she was looking at him like she was actually
waiting for an answer.
So he nodded. "Yeah. Sure."
Rebecca grinned. "See you then, genius."
Then she turned and walked away, and Jamey was left standing in the crisp night
air, his heart pounding, his world tilting.
Because for the first time, he wasn't just another face in the crowd.
For the first time, he wasn't invisible.
