Edinburgh
The front door clicked shut, and with it, the quiet warmth that had filled the
house for the last twenty-four hours vanished.
Rebecca stood in the hallway, the brush still in her hand, a faint streak of dried
blue paint still dusting her collarbone. She hadn't had time to scrub it all oﬀ. She
hadn't wanted to.
But as the heavy footsteps sounded in the entryway, she suddenly felt the urge to
scrape her own skin raw just to hide it.
Helen MacDonald didn't look like a woman who had spent the last two days
visiting a sick sister.
She looked perfect, her trench coat dry despite the Invermuir drizzle, her dark
hair pulled back into a knot so tight it seemed to pull the skin of her temples taut.
She set her bag down with a precise, heavy click.
"The place smells like wet wool," Helen said. She didn't look at Rebecca.
Her eyes drifted to the kitchen table, then to the floor where a stray smudge of
red paint, dry and stubborn, marked the floor near the wall. "And what is that?"
Rebecca dropped her hands into the pockets of her oversized hoodie, her fingers
curling into tight fists. "Just... an art project. For school."
Helen's gaze snapped to her. It was a cold, dissecting look, the kind that made
Rebecca feel six years old again, standing in her pink raincoat with a ruined test
in her hands.
"You have paint on your neck, Rebecca. And your hair is a bird's nest. Have you
spent the entire weekend doing 'projects' instead of looking at your mathematics
syllabus?"
"No," Rebecca said, her voice tighter than she wanted it to be. "I studied."
"Did you?" Helen walked past her, the scent of her expensive, sharp perfume
cutting through the lingering smell of rain and warmth.
She picked up the algebra folder from the kitchen counter, flipping through the
pages Jamey had neatly annotated.


Her thumb lingered on a small, handwritten note Jamey had left in the margin:
You've got this. Keep the signs straight.
Helen's eyes narrowed. "Whose writing is this?"
Rebecca's heart did a slow, violent roll in her chest. "A tutor. James Mitchell.
He's... Ms. MacPherson recommended him. He's the top of the advanced
chemistry class."
"A boy," Helen said. It wasn't a question. She threw the folder back onto the
counter. It landed with a dull slap.
"A boy is writing in your notebooks. A boy is spending his weekends here. Do you
think I'm stupid, Rebecca?"
"He was helping me," Rebecca whispered. "I'm passing now. I'm actually
understanding it."
"You are failing," Helen corrected, her voice dropping into that low, venomous
register that always made Rebecca's throat close up.
"I spoke with the headmaster's oﬃce on my way back. Your mid-term marks are a
disgrace. You're slipping. You're becoming lazy."
"You're behaving exactly like your father, thinking you can charm your way out
of responsibilities while someone else cleans up your mess."
The mention of her father felt like a physical blow.
Rebecca braced her shoulder against the wall, her knuckles white inside her
pockets.
"I work sixty hours a week so we can live in a decent place, so you can have a
future," Helen continued, her eyes fixed on Rebecca's face, watching for the first
sign of tears.
"And you spend your time dragging local boys into my house, painting the walls,
wasting my money. I won't have it. I won't watch you ruin yourself."
"I'm not ruining myself," Rebecca said, her voice cracking. "I'm trying."
"Then you'll try somewhere else," Helen said flatly. She turned her back, adjusting
the kettle on the stove.
"I've already called your Uncle Kenneth in Edinburgh. He has a room. There's a
structured academy near his estate."


"If your final algebra and chemistry marks next week are not absolute firsts, you
are leaving Invermuir. I am packing your things myself, and you will finish the
term in Edinburgh under his roof. No distractions. No local boys."
Rebecca's breath caught. The walls of the hallway seemed to lean inward,
pressing the air out of her lungs. "You can't do that."
"I can," Helen said, not looking up. "And I will."
"You have one week, Rebecca. Either you prove you can be a daughter I can be
proud of, or I will make sure you are put somewhere you have no choice but to
obey."
The kettle began to hiss. Rebecca stood frozen for three long seconds, the sound
filling her ears until it was a deafening roar.
She didn't say another word. She turned, grabbed her key from the hook, and
walked out the door.
The rain in Invermuir didn't fall; it hung in the air like a cold, stinging fog.
By the time Rebecca reached the turnoﬀ to Jamey's street, her hair was plastered
to her forehead, and her canvas shoes were soaked through, squelching with
every step.
Her chest ached with a cold, hollow panic.
She didn't think about what she was doing. She didn't think about Jamey's
parents, or what time it was, or how she must look.
She just knew she couldn't breathe in her own house.
She ran the last hundred yards, her feet crunching softly on the gravel driveway
of the small, white-walled cottage.
The lights in the kitchen were oﬀ, but a faint, warm glow filtered through the
curtains of the side window, Jamey's room.
She knocked. Softly at first, then harder when her shivering hands wouldn't
cooperate.A moment later, the lock clicked. The door swung open, and Jamey
stood there.
He was wearing his gray sweatpants and a faded black T-shirt, his hair messy
from sleep or study. He blinked into the darkness, and then his eyes went wide.
"Rebecca?"She didn't answer. She just stood there, her shoulders shaking, her
teeth chattering so hard she couldn't form a word.


Jamey didn't ask questions. He didn't look past her into the dark driveway.
He reached out, grabbed her by the wrists, and pulled her inside, shutting the
wooden door behind her with a swift, solid thud.
The hallway was warm, smelling of cedar and toast.
Jamey immediately dropped to his knees, untying her dripping shoes and sliding
them oﬀ her feet.He stood up, his hands finding her shoulders. She was trembling
so violently she could barely stand."Hey," he whispered, his voice low and steady.
"Hey. Look at me."She looked up, her hazel eyes glassy and dark.
Jamey reached for the zipper of her wet hoodie, pulling it down and sliding the
heavy, soaked fabric oﬀ her arms.
He tossed it onto the hallway radiator, then took her hands. They were ice-
cold.He rubbed them between his palms, his thumbs pressing hard into her skin
to bring the blood back."What happened? Did something happen?""She's... she's
back," Rebecca choked out, her voice small and ruined. "She found the notes. She
knows about you. Jamey, she's sending me back."Jamey stilled. "Back
where?""Edinburgh. She called her brother.""If I don't... if I don't get firsts on the
exams next week, she's packing my things. She's taking me away."A shadow
crossed Jamey's face, his jaw tightening so hard a small muscle jumped near his
ear. But his hands remained steady, warm and grounding. He didn't let go of her.
"Come here," he murmured.
He led her into his bedroom, closing the door
The room was small, dominated by a desk covered in textbooks and a bed pushed
against the wall under a heavy wool blanket.
He pulled the blanket back, guiding her to sit on the edge of the mattress.
Rebecca curled into herself, her knees drawn to her chest, her chin resting on her
arms.
"I can't go back, Jamey," she whispered to her knees. "I can't go back to
Edinburgh. I can't go back to the way it was before. I was... I was drowning
there."
Jamey sat beside her. He reached out and gently took her chin between his
fingers, tilting her face up.Her lips were pale, her cheeks damp with a mixture of
rain and tears."Look at me," he said.


She locked her eyes onto his.
"You're not going anywhere," Jamey said, his voice quiet but carrying a weight
that made her heart stutter.
"I promised you I'm not leaving. And I'm not letting her take you away, either."
"But the exams—"
"We have a week," Jamey interrupted. He moved closer, sliding his arms around
her waist, pulling her chest flush against his.
"A week is more than enough time. I'll teach you every line of that syllabus. I'll
make sure you know those equations better than Ms. MacPherson does."
Rebecca let out a shaky, broken breath.
She buried her face in the crook of his neck, her hands finding the hem of his T-
shirt, gripping the soft cotton like it was the only thing keeping her anchored to
the earth.
His skin was warm, smelling of soap and the dry heat of his room.
"Jamey," she breathed, her lips brushing his collarbone.
"I'm here," he whispered, his hands sliding up her back, tracing the curve of her
spine under her thin shirt. "I'm right here."
She pulled back just enough to look at him.
The panic in her chest hadn't vanished, but the coldness was gone, replaced by a
sudden, sharp heat that pooled in her stomach.
His gaze was dark, heavy with a protective, fierce intensity she had never seen in
him before.Rebecca reached up, her wet fingers tangling in his hair, pulling him
down.
Their lips met. It wasn't the soft, tentative kiss of the library wall, or the slow,
reverent touch of the couch.
It was hungry. Desperate. A silent plea against the dark, and Jamey answered it
instantly.He pressed her down onto the mattress, his body weight settling over
hers, solid and warm, shielding her from the rain outside and the shadow of
Edinburgh.
