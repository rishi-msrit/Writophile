The clock on Jamey's desk ticked past midnight, the soft sound lost beneath the
steady, rhythmic drumming of the rain against the windowpane.
Spread across the wooden surface were sheets of graph paper, two open
textbooks, and Rebecca's notebook, its pages filled with Jamey's neat annotations
and her own increasingly erratic pencil strokes.
Rebecca rubbed her temples, her copper curls falling forward, obscuring her
face."I don't get it," she muttered, her voice thick with frustration. "The signs...
they keep switching. Why does the charge invert when you move it across the
boundary?"
Jamey sat on the edge of his bed, watching the tense line of her shoulders. She
had been staring at the same page for forty minutes, her knuckles white around
her pencil.
The shadow of Edinburgh hung in the room, heavy and cold.It was in the way she
checked her phone every few minutes, as if expecting her mother's silent fury to
materialize in a text. It was in the desperate, sharp way she had kissed him when
she first arrived, wet and shivering, at his door.
Jamey stood up and walked over to her.
"You're overthinking it," he said softly, placing his hands on her shoulders.
She tensed for a second, then let out a long, ragged breath, letting her head drop
back against his chest. "I have to get a first, Jamey. If I don't—""You will," he
interrupted.
He slid his hands down her arms, finding her wrists. Her skin was warm now, a
contrast to the icy state she'd been in hours ago.
He pulled her chair back slightly, then bent down, catching her under the thighs
and lifting her before she could protest.
"Jamey—what are you doing?" she gasped, her hands flying to his neck for
balance.Instead of answering, he sat in her chair and settled her sideways across
his lap.
"Reducing the distance," he murmured, his face inches from hers.


Her hazel eyes widened, the frustration in them suddenly replaced by a quiet,
flustered heat. "We're supposed to be studying."
"We are," he said, wrapping one arm around her waist to keep her secure, his
hand resting on the bare skin of her lower back where her oversized shirt had
ridden up.
He picked up the pencil, placing it in her hand, then covered her fingers with his
own.
"Now. Look at the equation. The boundary isn't a wall, Rebecca. It's a transition.
When you cross it, you don't lose the value—you just change the perspective."
He guided her hand, drawing a clean, dark line through the numbers she had
scribbled over.
"See? The distance between the charges is what defines the force. The closer they
are, the stronger the bond."His voice was a low, steady rumble against her ear, his
breath warm against her temple. Rebecca swallowed, her focus wavering as his
thumb traced slow, deliberate circles against her hip."Jamey," she whispered, her
head tilting slightly toward his. "You're distracting me."
"Am I?" He smiled, his lips ghosting over the curve of her jaw. "Or am I helping
you focus?"
"Definitely distracting," she murmured, but she didn't pull away. Instead, she
leaned into him, her cheek resting against his collarbone, the scent of soap and
cedar wrapping around her like a shield."Let's test the theory," he whispered.
He set the pencil down. Slowly, his fingers traced a path up the inside of her
forearm, his touch feather-light, sending a shiver straight down her spine.
"Here," he said, tapping a spot near her wrist. "This is point A."
He slid his hand up, grazing her elbow, moving over the soft skin of her inner
arm until his fingers rested against the collarbone where the faint streak of blue
paint had been washed away."This is point B.""The distance between them is
fixed," he murmured, his gaze locking onto hers, dark and intense. "But if you
apply pressure..."
He pressed his thumb gently against the hollow of her collarbone, right over her
racing pulse.
Rebecca gasped softly, her lips parting."...the energy increases," he finished, his
voice dropping to a whisper."You're cheating," she breathed, her fingers tangling
in the hair at the back of his neck, pulling him down."I'm demonstrating," he
muttered, just before his mouth met hers.


The kiss was slow at first, a gentle reassurance against the panic that had chased
her through the rain. But as Rebecca's hands tightened in his hair, the tempo
shifted.
It became hungry, desperate, a silent rebellion against the miles her mother
wanted to put between them.
Jamey's hand slid up her back, under the hem of her hoodie, his warm palm
pressing against her bare skin. He pulled her closer, lifting her slightly until she
was straddling his lap, her knees framing his hips.
She moaned into the kiss, the sound swallowed by his mouth.
The distance between them was gone, replaced by a heat that made the cold
Highlands night outside feel like a distant memory.
When he finally pulled back, both of them were breathing heavily.Rebecca's
forehead rested against his, her eyes dark and blown wide. "If Ms. MacPherson
saw this, she'd fail us both."

He shifted, standing up with her still wrapped around him, and carried her the
short distance to the bed.
They tumbled onto the mattress, notebooks and loose papers fluttering to the
floor. Jamey hovered over her, his eyes scanning her face, looking for any trace of
the shadow that had darkened her eyes earlier."Better?" he asked softly.Rebecca
smiled, a genuine, soft thing that reached her eyes. She reached up, tracing the
line of his jaw with her fingers."Better," she admitted. "But we still have forty
equations to go."
"Then let's get to work," he said, but he didn't move. Instead, he leaned down and
kissed her again, slower this time, his hands sliding down to frame her waist.
For the next two hours, the room was filled with the soft murmur of formulas and
the quiet rustle of pages.
They didn't go back to the desk. Instead, they stayed on the bed, Rebecca sitting
between his legs, her back pressed against his chest as he went over the syllabus.
Every time she got a question right, he would reward her with a kiss—on her
shoulder, her neck, the side of her temple.
Every time she got frustrated, he would stop, pull her closer, and use his touch to
ground her until the tension drained from her shoulders.


By three in the morning, the pile of solved problems was higher than the blank
ones.
Rebecca stared at the final equation on her page, her pencil hovering.
She wrote down the final steps, her hand steady now."Done," she
whispered.Jamey leaned over her shoulder, checking the signs, the balancing, the
final value.
"Perfect," he murmured, a proud smile warming his eyes. "First-class marks."
Rebecca let the pencil drop. She turned in his arms, wrapping her hands around
his neck, her face glowing with a relief so intense it made her eyes sting.
"We did it," she said.
"You did it," Jamey corrected, pulling her down onto his chest.
They lay together in the quiet room, the rain outside slowing to a gentle drizzle.
Rebecca listened to the steady, calm rhythm of his heartbeat beneath her ear, her
fingers tracing idle patterns on his shirt.
"Jamey?"
"Yeah?""I'm not going to Edinburgh," she said, her voice carrying a quiet, fierce
determination.
Jamey wrapped his arms tighter around her, kissing the top of her head.
"I know," he whispered. "We're not letting them put any distance between us."
And in the quiet warmth of his room, with his arms holding her close, Rebecca
finally believed it.
